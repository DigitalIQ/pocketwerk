# Competitor analysis (anonymised)

The brief forbids naming other Dock apps anywhere in the project. Product names are left out on purpose; the notes describe patterns only. Source: manual search 2026-10-13 (no DataForSEO).

## How the result pages are built

| Pattern | Seen on |
|---|---|
| H1 names the job ("app folders / groups for your Dock") | most product pages |
| hero shows a big screenshot of a popup over a Dock | most product pages |
| price or trial in the hero | paid utilities |
| feature grid of 6 to 12 equal cards | most |
| FAQ on permissions and macOS version | some |
| "how to" guides that end in a product plug | guides |

## Common themes (the page needs them too)

Grouping apps in the Dock, one click to open, themes and looks, keyboard shortcuts, macOS version support.

## Gaps nobody answers well

1. How a pocket differs from a plain Dock folder stack (forum question, unanswered by product pages).
2. What leaves the Mac (privacy in plain words).
3. The first-launch warning for apps that are not notarized, step by step.
4. Free and open source as a reason to trust, with the source one click away.

## Patterns of the strong pages

- One clear sentence of what it does above the fold.
- The product shown at work, not described.
- Install path obvious, one button.

## Mistakes of the weak pages

- Feature walls of equal cards that all look the same.
- Purple-blue glow heroes that could be any app.
- Claims without proof (ratings, "loved by thousands").
